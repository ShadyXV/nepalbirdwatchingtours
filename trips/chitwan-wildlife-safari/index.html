Chitwan Jungle Birding and Wildlife Safari

Rhinos at dawn, hornbills overhead and a river full of kingfishers, all from one comfortable base.

Four relaxed days in Nepal's best-known lowland jungle. Krishna leads you on dugout canoe rides, jungle walks and jeep drives, looking for everything from the Greater One-horned Rhinoceros to kingfishers and hornbills. Chitwan is one of the richest bird areas in Asia, with sal forest, tall grassland and river and lake edges, all within easy reach of a single lodge in Sauraha. Mornings are early, afternoons are slow, and every day ends with a bird list and a cold drink.

Highlights

  • Dawn dugout-canoe drift on the Rapti River, with gharials, mugger crocodiles and kingfishers
  • Greater One-horned Rhinoceros on foot with a naturalist, and from the safety of a jeep
  • Great and Oriental Pied Hornbills in the sal forest canopy
  • Bis Hazari Tal, a Ramsar-listed lake with jacanas, storks and fish-eagles
  • Tall grasslands of Kasara, home of the Bengal Florican's spring display
  • An evening among the Tharu people, with a stick dance and a local meal

The way, day by day

  1. Day 1Sauraha

    Day 1→

    150 m · 3 night(s) · lodge

    1. Stay: lodge. Garden lodge in Sauraha on the edge of the park; three nights in the same room

      Our base for three nights on the north edge of the park. The lodge gardens are good for bee-eaters, barbets and sunbirds, and the Rapti River is a short walk away.

    2. A gentle afternoon walk to meet the elephants and their calves, run by the park. The quiet sal edges around the compound are a good first look at the forest birds.

    26 birds possible

    Birds: Oriental Magpie-Robin, Red-vented Bulbul, Black Drongo, Common Myna, Asian Pied Starling, Green Bee-eater, Rufous Treepie, Common Tailorbird, Coppersmith Barbet, Rose-ringed Parakeet, Indian Roller, Lineated Barbet, Spotted Owlet, Greater Coucal, Long-tailed Shrike, Common Iora, Black-hooded Oriole, Common Hoopoe, Red-breasted Parakeet, Black-crested Bulbul, Asian Koel, Crimson Sunbird, Cattle Egret, Indian Peafowl, Greater Racket-tailed Drongo, Red Junglefowl

    Mammals: Rhesus Macaque, Asian Elephant

    Plants: Silk-cotton Tree, Sal

    Butterflies: Common Mormon

  2. Day 2Baghmara Community Forest

    Day 2→

    150 m

    1. We slip into a long dugout canoe in the cool of dawn and drift downriver in near silence. Gharials sun themselves on the sandbars, kingfishers hunt from the snags, and sometimes a rhinoceros wades in the shallows at the other bank.

    2. A community-run buffer-zone forest where we walk slowly with a local naturalist, listening for woodpeckers, hornbills and pittas. Rhinoceros and Spotted Deer are regular, and we keep a respectful distance from the big animals.

  3. Day 3Tharu Village, Sauraha

    Day 3→

    150 m

    1. A full-day jeep drive into the heart of the national park, via the Kasara headquarters. The great grasslands here are the home of the Bengal Florican, and the sal forest and wetlands bring hornbills, vultures and, with luck, a glimpse of the elusive big animals.

    2. An easy late-afternoon walk through a Tharu village with mud-and-thatch houses and golden fields. Weaver nests hang in the trees and the evening ends with a local meal and a stick dance.

    38 birds possible

    Mammals: ★ Spotted Deer, Wild Boar, Rhesus Macaque, ★ Greater One-horned Rhinoceros, ★ Hog Deer, Northern Plains Gray Langur, Sambar, Gaur, Sloth Bear, Bengal Tiger, Leopard

    Birds: Red Junglefowl, Indian Peafowl, Plain Prinia, Common Iora, ★ Black Francolin, Striated Grassbird, Chestnut-capped Babbler, Crested Serpent-Eagle, Oriental Pied Hornbill, Great Hornbill, Swamp Francolin, Egyptian Vulture, Oriental Honey-buzzard, Lesser Adjutant, Black-necked Stork, Woolly-necked Stork, ★ Bengal Florican, Pied Harrier, Greater Spotted Eagle, White-rumped Vulture, Jerdon's Babbler, Baya Weaver, Black Drongo, Common Myna, Asian Pied Starling, Cattle Egret, Indian Pond-Heron, Red-wattled Lapwing, Scaly-breasted Munia, Indian Roller, Long-tailed Shrike, Common Hoopoe, Spotted Owlet, Rose-ringed Parakeet, White-throated Kingfisher, Green Bee-eater, Shikra, Citrine Wagtail

    Reptiles: Gharial, Bengal Monitor, Mugger Crocodile, Indian Rock Python

    Plants: Kans Grass

    Butterflies: Plain Tiger, Common Rose, Lemon Pansy

  4. Day 4Bis Hazari Tal

    Day 4→

    190 m

    1. The "twenty thousand lakes" of Bis Hazari, a Ramsar-listed wetland in the community forest north of the park. A last dawn walk around the shore gives jacanas, storks, kingfishers and, if we are lucky, rhinoceros bathing in the shallows.

    28 birds possible

    Birds: Lesser Whistling-Duck, Little Grebe, Grey-headed Swamphen, White-breasted Waterhen, Common Moorhen, Bronze-winged Jacana, Great Egret, Little Cormorant, Indian Pond-Heron, White-throated Kingfisher, Cotton Pygmy-Goose, Indian Spot-billed Duck, Purple Heron, Oriental Darter, Asian Openbill, Woolly-necked Stork, Pheasant-tailed Jacana, Oriental Pied Hornbill, Black-necked Stork, Painted Stork, Lesser Adjutant, Grey-headed Fish-Eagle, Great Hornbill, Stork-billed Kingfisher, Gadwall, Northern Pintail, Ferruginous Duck, Brown Fish-Owl

    Reptiles: Mugger Crocodile, Bengal Monitor

    Mammals: Greater One-horned Rhinoceros, Spotted Deer, Sambar, Wild Boar, Bengal Tiger
