Chitwan Jungle Birding and Wildlife Safari

Rhinos at dawn, hornbills overhead and a river full of kingfishers, all from one comfortable base.

4 days and 111 birds recorded on this route.

Best months: October to March.

Start the journeyDay by day, on the map

1 / 6

Four relaxed days in Nepal's best-known lowland jungle, all from one lodge in Sauraha. Krishna takes you by dugout canoe, on foot and by jeep through sal forest, tall grassland and the banks of the Rapti: kingfishers, hornbills, storks and, in spring, the Bengal Florican, with rhinos along the way. Easy walking and early mornings; good for first-timers and photographers alike.

With guests

  • March 2026, Kasara and the Core GrasslandsWaiting for the florican

    with Placeholder Guest D and friends, Australia

    In March the grass at Kasara is shorter after the annual cutting, and if you are lucky, you can find the Bengal Florican in its display. We left the lodge in the dark and sat in the jeep at the edge of the grassland with the engine off, a thermos of tea passing between us.

    For almost an hour there was just the grass, the dawn chorus, and the cold. Then a black-and-white shape shot up out of the field, wings quivering, hung in the air for a moment and dropped back out of sight. My guests laughed out loud. It was the sort of thing you can only appreciate by seeing it.

    Seen that day: Bengal Florican, Spotted Deer, Hog Deer, Black Francolin, Greater One-horned Rhinoceros

  • February 2026, Rapti RiverDrifting past a rhinoceros

    with Placeholder Guest C and son, United Kingdom

    The Rapti is at its best in the cold hour after sunrise, when mist still lies on the water and the canoeman hardly needs to paddle. We had seen a Gharial on a sandbar, a Pied Kingfisher hovering over its pool, and a pair of Ruddy Shelducks on a spit, when I saw a grey shape at the edge of the tall grass.

    A Greater One-horned Rhinoceros stood knee-deep and was feeding, quite undisturbed by our silence. My guest's son, who was eleven, held his breath for what felt like a whole minute. I think he decided that morning that he wanted to become a naturalist.

    Seen that day: Greater One-horned Rhinoceros, Gharial, Pied Kingfisher, Ruddy Shelduck, Mugger Crocodile

  • February 2026, Baghmara Community ForestThe sound of a hornbill

    with Placeholder Guests M and N, Switzerland

    We were walking the sal forest in single file when I heard it before anyone else: a rhythmic whoosh, like a steam train a long way off. A Great Hornbill was crossing the forest overhead and its wings were beating the air with each stroke.

    It landed on a bare branch and began to feed on a fig, a huge yellow casque and a bill like a banana. My guests hardly dared to breathe. A pair of Oriental Pied Hornbills joined it, and we stood there for twenty minutes with the forest all to ourselves.

    Seen that day: Great Hornbill, Oriental Pied Hornbill, Red Junglefowl, Indian Peafowl, Lesser Necklaced Laughingthrush

A day on this trip

We go out at six, on the river or in the forest while it's cool, and come back for lunch and a rest through the heat of the day. At about three we head out again until dusk; the walks are flat and slow, with plenty of waiting for the light.

The way, day by day

  1. Day 1
    Sauraha150 m · Oriental Magpie-Robin, Red-vented Bulbul

    1. Our base for three nights on the north edge of the park. The lodge gardens are good for bee-eaters, barbets and sunbirds, and the Rapti River is a short walk away.

    2. A gentle afternoon walk to meet the elephants and their calves, run by the park. The quiet sal edges around the compound are a good first look at the forest birds.

    Birds 26: Oriental Magpie-Robin, Red-vented Bulbul, Black Drongo, Common Myna, Asian Pied Starling, Green Bee-eater, Rufous Treepie, Common Tailorbird, Coppersmith Barbet, Rose-ringed Parakeet, Indian Roller, Lineated Barbet, Spotted Owlet, Greater Coucal, Long-tailed Shrike, Common Iora, Black-hooded Oriole, Common Hoopoe, Red-breasted Parakeet, Black-crested Bulbul, Asian Koel, Crimson Sunbird, Cattle Egret, Indian Peafowl, Greater Racket-tailed Drongo, Red Junglefowl

    Mammals 2: Rhesus Macaque, Asian Elephant

    Butterflies 1: Common Mormon

    Plants 2: Silk-cotton Tree, Sal

  2. Day 2
    Rapti River · Baghmara Community Forest150 m · Ruddy Shelduck, Pied Kingfisher

    1. We slip into a long dugout canoe in the cool of dawn and drift downriver in near silence. Gharials sun themselves on the sandbars, kingfishers hunt from the snags, and sometimes a rhinoceros wades in the shallows at the other bank.

    2. A community-run buffer-zone forest where we walk slowly with a local naturalist, listening for woodpeckers, hornbills and pittas. Rhinoceros and Spotted Deer are regular, and we keep a respectful distance from the big animals.

  3. Day 3
    Kasara and the Core Grasslands · Tharu Village, Sauraha150 m · Black Francolin, Bengal Florican

    1. A full-day jeep drive into the heart of the national park, via the Kasara headquarters. The great grasslands here are the home of the Bengal Florican, and the sal forest and wetlands bring hornbills, vultures and, with luck, a glimpse of the elusive big animals.

    2. An easy late-afternoon walk through a Tharu village with mud-and-thatch houses and golden fields. Weaver nests hang in the trees and the evening ends with a local meal and a stick dance.

  4. Day 4
    Bis Hazari Tal190 m · Pheasant-tailed Jacana, Lesser Whistling-Duck

    1. The "twenty thousand lakes" of Bis Hazari, a Ramsar-listed wetland in the community forest north of the park. A last dawn walk around the shore gives jacanas, storks, kingfishers and, if we are lucky, rhinoceros bathing in the shallows.
