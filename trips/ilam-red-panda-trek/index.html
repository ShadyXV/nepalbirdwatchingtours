Red Panda Trek

Ten days in the bamboo cloud forest of eastern Nepal, searching for the red panda at dawn and dusk.

10 days, up to 3,300 m, and 68 birds recorded on this route.

Best months: March to May and October to December.

Start the journeyDay by day, on the map

1 / 5

Ten days in the bamboo and rhododendron cloud forest of far-eastern Nepal, searching for the red panda with a local tracker in the first and last light. Between searches the forest is full of eastern birds: Satyr Tragopan, Fire-tailed Myzornis, parrotbills and Scarlet Finch. For patient photographers and birders who are happy on a quiet, remote trail.

Birds to look for

None of them is promised: wild birds keep their own plans.

  • Golden-throated Barbet

    Psilopogon franklinii

    Ilam · 1,200 m · usually seen

    “A colourful hill-forest barbet with a gold, red and black head.”

  • Satyr Tragopan

    Tragopan satyra

    Dobate · 2,660 m · sometimes seen

    “A crimson, pearl-spotted forest pheasant that is heard long before it is seen, so every glimpse is a celebration.”

  • Scarlet Finch

    Carpodacus sipahi

    Dobate · 2,660 m · sometimes seen

    “A brilliant scarlet male in a dark forest is something you never forget.”

  • Black-throated Parrotbill

    Suthora nipalensis

    Dobate · 2,660 m · usually seen

    “A tiny orange-and-black parrotbill that whirrs through the bamboo in busy little parties.”

  • Hoary-throated Barwing

    Actinodura nipalensis

    Sandakpur Ridge Search Area · 3,300 m · usually seen

    “A barred, hoary-throated babbler working the mossy limbs.”

  • Fire-tailed Myzornis

    Myzornis pyrrhoura

    Sandakpur Ridge Search Area · 3,300 m · sometimes seen

    “At the rhododendron flowers”

  • Golden-breasted Fulvetta

    Lioparus chrysotis

    Dobate · 2,660 m · usually seen

    “A little jewel of black, gold and white in the bamboo thicket.”

  • Red-headed Bullfinch

    Pyrrhula erythrocephala

    Red Panda Habitat, Sandakpur Ridge · 3,100 m · usually seen

    Red Panda Habitat, Sandakpur Ridge · 3,100 m · sometimes seen

A day on this trip

We search the red panda forest in the first and last hours of light, with a local tracker reading the signs, and bird the bamboo in between. The days are slow and patient: three to five hours of walking at most, and plenty of time with the camera.

The way, day by day

  1. Day 1
    Kathmandu1,400 m · Black Kite, Large-billed Crow

    1. Airport pickup, hotel check-in, and a photography and trekking briefing.

    Birds 10: Black Kite, Large-billed Crow, Common Myna, Oriental Magpie-Robin, Red-vented Bulbul, Spotted Dove, Himalayan Bulbul, Eurasian Kestrel, Grey Wagtail, Egyptian Vulture

    Seen from here: Phulchowki (2,782 m)

  2. Day 2
    Ilam1,200 m · Golden-throated Barbet, Cattle Egret

    1. A morning flight to Bhadrapur, in the far eastern lowlands of Nepal.

    2. Then the drive into the tea-growing hills of Ilam. The tea gardens and forest patches around town are a good first look at the birds of the eastern hills.

    Birds 25: Black Kite, Cattle Egret, Asian Pied Starling, Common Myna, Indian Pond-Heron, Black Drongo, Red-wattled Lapwing, Indian Roller, Spotted Owlet, White-throated Kingfisher, Green Bee-eater, Himalayan Bulbul, Red-vented Bulbul, Great Barbet, Grey Treepie, Oriental White-eye, Long-tailed Shrike, Golden-throated Barbet, Blue-throated Barbet, Verditer Flycatcher, Black Bulbul, Common Tailorbird, Long-tailed Minivet, Grey-hooded Warbler, Mountain Hawk-Eagle

    Mammals 1: Assamese Macaque

  3. Day 3
    Dobate2,660 m · Satyr Tragopan, Scarlet Finch

    1. We drive to the trailhead and begin trekking into forested hill terrain, up to the small village of Dobate on the edge of the red panda forest.

    Birds 15: Rufous Sibia, Whiskered Yuhina, Blue-winged Minla, Green-backed Tit, Chestnut-crowned Laughingthrush, Kalij Pheasant, Darjeeling Woodpecker, Rufous-bellied Niltava, Large Niltava, Golden-breasted Fulvetta, Yellow-billed Blue Magpie, Black-throated Parrotbill, Hill Partridge, Satyr Tragopan, Scarlet Finch

    Mammals 3: Barking Deer, Red Panda, Yellow-throated Marten

    Plants 2: Maling Bamboo, Tree Rhododendron

  4. Day 4
    Bamboo Forest Camp2,950 m · Hoary-throated Barwing, Fire-tailed Myzornis

    1. The first of two days of trekking through dense bamboo-rhododendron forest, the preferred habitat of red pandas.

    Birds 15: White-browed Fulvetta, Rufous-vented Tit, Stripe-throated Yuhina, Chestnut-tailed Minla, Black-faced Laughingthrush, Black-throated Parrotbill, Hoary-throated Barwing, Spotted Laughingthrush, Rufous-vented Yuhina, Darjeeling Woodpecker, Red-headed Bullfinch, Satyr Tragopan, Great Parrotbill, Fire-tailed Myzornis, Gold-naped Finch

    Mammals 3: Himalayan Langur, Red Panda, Himalayan Black Bear

    Plants 2: Maling Bamboo, Bearded Rhododendron

  5. Day 5
    Red Panda Habitat, Sandakpur Ridge3,100 m · Blood Pheasant, Grey-crested Tit

    1. We reach the core habitat, with naturalist guides tracking signs (droppings, feeding marks) to locate active areas. This is our base for the search days.

    Birds 18: White-browed Fulvetta, Grey-crested Tit, Rufous-vented Tit, Black-faced Laughingthrush, Large-billed Crow, Golden-breasted Fulvetta, Black-throated Parrotbill, Red-headed Bullfinch, Dark-breasted Rosefinch, Collared Grosbeak, Spotted Laughingthrush, Darjeeling Woodpecker, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Satyr Tragopan, Blood Pheasant, Himalayan Monal, Fire-tailed Myzornis, Scarlet Finch

    Mammals 4: Yellow-throated Marten, Himalayan Serow, Red Panda, Himalayan Black Bear

    Plants 2: Maling Bamboo, Bell-flowered Rhododendron

  6. Day 6
    Sandakpur Ridge Search Area3,300 m · Grandala, Himalayan Griffon

    1. Three dedicated days based in the core habitat zone, conducting early morning and late afternoon searches, the peak activity windows for red pandas, alongside forest birding for pheasants, laughingthrushes and raptors.

    Birds 14: Black-faced Laughingthrush, Spotted Laughingthrush, Hoary-throated Barwing, Himalayan Griffon, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Large-billed Crow, Satyr Tragopan, Blood Pheasant, Himalayan Monal, Hill Partridge, Black Eagle, Lammergeier, Grandala, Fire-tailed Myzornis

    Mammals 2: Himalayan Serow, Red Panda

    Plants 1: Tree Rhododendron

    Seen from here: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), Kanchenjunga Himal (0 m)

  7. Day 7
    Rest day at Sandakpur Ridge Search Area3,300 m · Satyr Tragopan, Hoary-throated Barwing

    Birds 14: Black-faced Laughingthrush, Spotted Laughingthrush, Hoary-throated Barwing, Himalayan Griffon, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Large-billed Crow, Satyr Tragopan, Blood Pheasant, Himalayan Monal, Hill Partridge, Black Eagle, Lammergeier, Grandala, Fire-tailed Myzornis

    Mammals 2: Himalayan Serow, Red Panda

    Plants 1: Tree Rhododendron

    Seen from here: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), Kanchenjunga Himal (0 m)

  8. Day 8
    Rest day at Sandakpur Ridge Search Area3,300 m · Satyr Tragopan, Hoary-throated Barwing

    Birds 14: Black-faced Laughingthrush, Spotted Laughingthrush, Hoary-throated Barwing, Himalayan Griffon, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Large-billed Crow, Satyr Tragopan, Blood Pheasant, Himalayan Monal, Hill Partridge, Black Eagle, Lammergeier, Grandala, Fire-tailed Myzornis

    Mammals 2: Himalayan Serow, Red Panda

    Plants 1: Tree Rhododendron

    Seen from here: Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), Kanchenjunga Himal (0 m)

  9. Day 9
    Kathmandu1,400 m · Black Kite, Cattle Egret

    1. Down from the forest and back by road through Ilam to Bhadrapur, for the flight to Kathmandu.

    2. The return journey ends with a night back in Kathmandu.

    Birds 19: Black Kite, Cattle Egret, Asian Pied Starling, Common Myna, Indian Pond-Heron, Black Drongo, Red-wattled Lapwing, Indian Roller, Spotted Owlet, White-throated Kingfisher, Green Bee-eater, Large-billed Crow, Oriental Magpie-Robin, Red-vented Bulbul, Spotted Dove, Himalayan Bulbul, Eurasian Kestrel, Grey Wagtail, Egyptian Vulture

    Seen from here: Phulchowki (2,782 m)

    Kathmandu1,400 m · Black Kite, Large-billed Crow

    1. Final breakfast and the departure transfer.

    Seen from here: Phulchowki (2,782 m)
