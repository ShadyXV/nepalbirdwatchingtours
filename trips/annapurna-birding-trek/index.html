Annapurna Sanctuary Birding Trek

Twelve days from warm river valleys to the foot of Annapurna, listening for every voice in the forest.

12 days, up to 4,130 m, and 110 birds recorded on this route.

Best months: March to May and October to November.

Start the journeyDay by day, on the map

1 / 6

The classic Annapurna Sanctuary trek, planned around the birds. Krishna walks with you and your trek guide from warm river valleys through oak, rhododendron and bamboo forest to the snow at 4,130 m, from Brown Dippers on the Modi Khola to Snow Pigeons at the base camp. For fit walkers who are happy to start early and stop often.

With guests

  • November 2025, BambooWaiting for the parrotbill

    with Placeholder Guest B and a friend, Netherlands

    We reached Bamboo wet and tired after the long walk down from the Sanctuary. I told my guests I had heard something moving in the bamboo behind the teahouse, and they were too tired to argue. We stood in the drizzle for half an hour, watching a flock of fulvettas and a Golden Bush-Robin, and then it appeared.

    A Great Parrotbill is a big, soft, brown bird with a stubby yellow bill, and it came out onto a bamboo stem and calmly started stripping shoots, two metres away. My guest forgot his tiredness completely. He later said it was the bird of the trek, more than the snow peaks.

    Seen that day: Great Parrotbill, Golden-breasted Fulvetta, Himalayan Bluetail, Golden Bush-Robin, White-browed Fulvetta

  • November 2025, Annapurna Base CampSnow pigeons at the end of the world

    with Placeholder Guests I and J, United States

    It was minus eight when we crept out of the lodge for sunrise at Annapurna Base Camp, and nobody spoke. The south face of Annapurna I went from grey to pink to gold, and then a flock of about forty Snow Pigeons came up out of the dark cliffs and crossed it. I will never forget the sound of the wingbeats in that silence.

    Later, on the moraine, an Alpine Accentor hopped almost to my guests' boots, and a Large-eared Pika called from the boulders. My guests said it was the coldest and happiest morning of their lives.

    Seen that day: Snow Pigeon, Lammergeier, Alpine Accentor, Blue Sheep, Large-eared Pika, Yellow-billed Chough

  • April 2025, TadapaniThe pheasant that walked into breakfast

    with Placeholder Guest A and partner, Germany

    We had reached Tadapani the evening before in thick cloud, and I had told my guests only that morning would show us what the place was. At first light I woke them for a cup of tea on the doorstep. The clearing was grey and still, and then, from the ravine below, a Himalayan Monal began to call.

    We waited, tea going cold, until a Kalij Pheasant stepped out of the rhododendrons and walked straight across the open ground with its tail low. Right behind it the sun reached the ridge, and the blooms along the trail turned scarlet all at once. A Fire-tailed Sunbird dropped into the nearest flower, so close we could see the red in its tail.

    My guests said nothing for a full minute. That, to me, is the best sort of silence.

    Seen that day: Kalij Pheasant, Himalayan Monal, Blue-fronted Redstart, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Tree Rhododendron, Himalayan Langur

  • April 2025, Poon HillA wall of mountains and one very loud monal

    with Placeholder Guests G and H, Canada

    We left Ghorepani at four in the morning with headlamps, climbing in the dark behind a line of other trekkers. I always warn my guests that a bird is as good a reason to climb as the view. Halfway up, somewhere below us in the black forest, a Himalayan Monal began to call.

    By the time we reached the top the sky had gone pink. Dhaulagiri stood on the left and Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre were catching the first light, each one turning gold in turn. Behind us a Lammergeier slid along the ridge, long-winged and rust-coloured, and my guests, who had come for the mountains, spent the next ten minutes with their heads tilted back.

    Seen that day: Himalayan Monal, Lammergeier, Rufous-breasted Accentor, Spotted Nutcracker

  • April 2025The last evening's list

    with Placeholder Guests K and L, Australia

    On the final night in Ghandruk, with the slate roofs still warm from the sun, we sat on the guesthouse terrace and went through the week's list. It had started with Brown Dippers at the river by Birethanti, and it ended with a Himalayan Monal, Great Barbets, forktails and a hillside of rhododendron.

    My guests were most pleased with the small ones, a sunbird on a flower, a bulbul on a wall. A trek like this, I tell people, is a long walk in the company of friends who happen to have wings.

    Seen that day: Himalayan Monal, Brown Dipper, Great Barbet, Spotted Forktail, Tree Rhododendron

A day on this trip

We're out at first light for an hour of birding before breakfast, then walk four to six hours at a birder's pace, stopping for every flock. Afternoons are short, so there's time to photograph, sketch, or sit with tea while the mountains turn pink.

The way, day by day

  1. Day 1
    Tikhedhunga1,540 m · Brown Dipper, Spotted Forktail

    1. A relaxed hour at dawn on the lakeshore before the drive to Nayapul. Kingfishers, cormorants and egrets are easy warm-up birds, with the Annapurna range floating above the water on a clear morning.

    2. The trek proper begins where the Bhurungdi and Modi Khola meet. The bridge and boulders are a great place for dippers, redstarts and wagtails before the first climb.

    3. First night under the stars of the Annapurna foothills. The evening walk along the stream above the teahouse often turns up minivets, sunbirds and the first Himalayan Bulbuls.

    2. Two nights at Ghorepani give us a full day to bird the old rhododendron forest around the village. This is classic Himalayan pheasant country, with monals, laughingthrushes and a great mixed-flock bird list.

  5. Day 5
    Chhomrong2,170 m · Grey-headed Canary-Flycatcher, Little Forktail

    1. The long descent to this clear mountain stream is a mid-day rest for the birds as well as us. Forktails and redstarts work the wet rocks while minivets move through the trees overhead.

    2. The last real village before the Sanctuary, spread over a hillside of stone steps with Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre across the valley. We bird the gardens and forest edge here on the evening of arrival and again on the way down.

  6. Day 6
    Dovan2,600 m · Rufous-vented Yuhina, Chestnut-crowned Warbler

    1. The trail drops to a river then climbs to Sinuwa, the doorway to the Modi Khola gorge. Birds come easily here at mid-morning as the clouds lift from the forest.

    2. A damp, green gorge where bamboo walls the trail and the river is always roaring. Pheasants and partridges call at dawn and Himalayan Langur crash through the trees.

    1. Via the Himalaya teahouse (2,920 m), we reach Deurali at the tree line. Here Himalayan Monals and Blood Pheasants share the slope with Himalayan Tahr on the cliffs, and the forest gives way to rhododendron scrub and rock.

  8. Day 8
    Machhapuchhre Base Camp · Annapurna Base Camp4,130 m · Red-billed Chough, Himalayan Snowcock

    1. We enter the Sanctuary through a gap between Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre. Snow Pigeons wheel along the cliffs and Blue Sheep graze the grass, an hour or two of easy walking before the final steps to Annapurna Base Camp.

    2. The end of the climb: a vast bowl of ice with Annapurna I, Annapurna South, Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre all around. We watch sunset and then dawn here, with Snow Pigeons, Lammergeier and Alpine Accentors for company.

  10. Day 10
    Jhinu Danda1,780 m · Ultramarine Flycatcher, Brown Dipper

    1. We drop back into warm, leafy forest and walk down to the natural hot springs on the Modi Khola. The river bank here is lovely for forktails, redstarts and the occasional Crested Kingfisher.

  11. Day 11
    Ghandruk1,940 m · Spotted Dove, Rose-ringed Parakeet

    1. A Gurung village on the hillside above the Modi Khola and a long lunch stop. The gardens and field edges are good for sunbirds, bulbuls and flycatchers.

    2. A final night in one of Nepal's loveliest Gurung villages, with slate roofs and stone lanes. The oak forest on the edge of Ghandruk is great for a last morning flock, with Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre in the background.

  12. Day 12
    Nayapul1,070 m · White Wagtail, Brown Dipper

    1. The last real birding stop before the road, as the trail comes down to the valley floor and the river forest. Wagtails, kingfishers and flycatchers keep the walking leisurely.

    2. The road end where we began. A cup of tea, a last look at the river, and a few last bird ticks before the transfer back to Pokhara.

    Birds 14: Grey Wagtail, White Wagtail, Red-vented Bulbul, White-throated Kingfisher, Plumbeous Water Redstart, Brown Dipper, Verditer Flycatcher, Black Bulbul, Great Barbet, Crested Kingfisher, Common Kingfisher, Spotted Forktail, Black Kite, Oriental Magpie-Robin
