Annapurna Sanctuary Birding Trek

Twelve days from warm river valleys to the foot of Annapurna, listening for every voice in the forest.

This is the classic Annapurna Base Camp trek, planned around the birds. Krishna walks with you and your trek guide, calling out the song of a Himalayan Monal before sunrise and finding the Great Parrotbill in the bamboo. You climb from subtropical valleys through oak and rhododendron forest to the alpine amphitheatre ringed by Annapurna I, Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre. Mornings and evenings are for birds and sunrise, and days are paced so everyone, birder or not, can enjoy the walk.

Highlights

  • Dawn from Poon Hill, with Himalayan Monal calling below the Dhaulagiri and Annapurna ranges
  • Rhododendron forest in bloom at Ghorepani and Tadapani (March and April)
  • Great Parrotbill, laughingthrushes and fulvettas in the bamboo above Chhomrong
  • Snow Pigeon, Lammergeier and Blue Sheep at Annapurna Base Camp, 4,130 m
  • Brown Dipper and forktails on the Modi Khola, then a soak in the Jhinu Danda hot springs
  • A long list of babblers, flycatchers and sunbirds that you only meet by walking slowly

The way, day by day

  1. Day 1Tikhedhunga

    Day 1→

    1,540 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. Stay: hotel. Night before the trek in a Lakeside hotel

      A relaxed hour at dawn on the lakeshore before the drive to Nayapul. Kingfishers, cormorants and egrets are easy warm-up birds, with the Annapurna range floating above the water on a clear morning.

    2. The trek proper begins where the Bhurungdi and Modi Khola meet. The bridge and boulders are a great place for dippers, redstarts and wagtails before the first climb.

    3. Stay: teahouse. Simple teahouse at the foot of the Ulleri stairs; hot meals and dorm or twin rooms

      First night under the stars of the Annapurna foothills. The evening walk along the stream above the teahouse often turns up minivets, sunbirds and the first Himalayan Bulbuls.

    37 birds possible

    Birds: Little Cormorant, Indian Pond-Heron, Cattle Egret, White-throated Kingfisher, Red-vented Bulbul, Black Kite, Oriental Magpie-Robin, Pied Kingfisher, Common Kingfisher, Little Egret, Red-wattled Lapwing, Pied Bushchat, Great Cormorant, Plumbeous Water Redstart, Grey Wagtail, Himalayan Bulbul, Oriental White-eye, Brown Dipper, Blue Whistling-Thrush, White-capped Redstart, Great Barbet, Verditer Flycatcher, Scarlet Minivet, Spotted Forktail, Crested Kingfisher, Ashy Drongo, Black Bulbul, Mountain Bulbul, Rufous Sibia, Long-tailed Minivet, Grey Treepie, Black-throated Sunbird, Rufous-bellied Niltava, Grey-hooded Warbler, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Crested Serpent-Eagle, Wedge-tailed Green-Pigeon

    Butterflies: Common Mormon, Northern Jungle Queen

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m)

  2. Day 2Ghorepani

    Day 2→

    2,860 m · 2 night(s) · teahouse

    1. After the famous stone staircase the forest changes: mossy oaks, the first rhododendrons and mixed flocks of babblers and warblers moving through at eye level.

    2. Stay: teahouse. Teahouse in Ghorepani with a sun terrace; two nights so the second day can be spent birding the forest

      Two nights at Ghorepani give us a full day to bird the old rhododendron forest around the village. This is classic Himalayan pheasant country, with monals, laughingthrushes and a great mixed-flock bird list.

    39 birds possible

    Birds: Whiskered Yuhina, Rufous Sibia, Great Barbet, Grey-hooded Warbler, Black-throated Tit, Blue-winged Minla, Chestnut-tailed Minla, White-throated Laughingthrush, Streaked Laughingthrush, Rufous-bellied Niltava, Long-tailed Minivet, Eurasian Jay, Yellow-bellied Fantail, Spotted Nutcracker, Rufous-winged Fulvetta, Hoary-throated Barwing, Himalayan Bluetail, Large Hawk-Cuckoo, Rufous-breasted Accentor, Rufous-vented Tit, Grey-crested Tit, Blue-fronted Redstart, Stripe-throated Yuhina, Himalayan Monal, Koklass Pheasant, Spotted Laughingthrush, Black-faced Laughingthrush, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Bar-tailed Treecreeper, Rufous-gorgeted Flycatcher, Himalayan Griffon, Hill Partridge, Great Parrotbill, White-collared Blackbird, Grey-winged Blackbird, Collared Grosbeak, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Satyr Tragopan, Himalayan Cuckoo

    Plants: Tree Rhododendron, Bearded Rhododendron, Necklace Orchid

    Mammals: Himalayan Langur

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m)

  3. Day 3Poon Hill

    Day 3→

    3,210 m

    1. A pre-dawn climb in the dark with headlamps, then sunrise over a wall of mountains. Dhaulagiri, Tukuche, Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre light up one after another, with monals sometimes calling on the slope below.

    12 birds possible

    Birds: ★ Himalayan Monal, ★ Rufous-breasted Accentor, ★ Spotted Nutcracker, Blue-fronted Redstart, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Himalayan Griffon, ★ Lammergeier, Koklass Pheasant, Great Parrotbill, Spotted Laughingthrush, Golden Eagle, Alpine Accentor

    Plants: Bearded Rhododendron

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m)

  4. Day 4Tadapani

    Day 4→

    2,630 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. The pass between Ghorepani and the Kimrong valley. Walking slowly through the cloud forest here gives some of the trek's best bamboo and rhododendron birds.

    2. Stay: teahouse. Teahouse at the forest edge, with a view of Machhapuchhre and Annapurna South

      A quiet forest clearing with rhododendrons right up to the doors. Dawn here is the best of the trek for pheasants, with Kalij and Himalayan Monal both regular around the edge of the clearing.

    24 birds possible

    Birds: Great Parrotbill, Rufous-breasted Accentor, Golden Bush-Robin, Rufous-vented Tit, Spotted Laughingthrush, Black-faced Laughingthrush, ★ Fire-tailed Sunbird, Rufous-gorgeted Flycatcher, Koklass Pheasant, ★ Himalayan Monal, Blood Pheasant, Gold-naped Finch, Dark-breasted Rosefinch, Collared Grosbeak, ★ Blue-fronted Redstart, Streaked Laughingthrush, Whiskered Yuhina, Grey-crested Tit, ★ Kalij Pheasant, Hill Partridge, Bar-tailed Treecreeper, White-collared Blackbird, Lammergeier, Satyr Tragopan

    Plants: Bell-flowered Rhododendron, ★ Tree Rhododendron, Bearded Rhododendron

    Mammals: ★ Himalayan Langur, Yellow-throated Marten

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m)

  5. Day 5Chhomrong

    Day 5→

    2,170 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. The long descent to this clear mountain stream is a mid-day rest for the birds as well as us. Forktails and redstarts work the wet rocks while minivets move through the trees overhead.

    2. Stay: teahouse. Gurung-run teahouse on the stepped hillside, with a view of Machhapuchhre and Annapurna South

      The last real village before the Sanctuary, spread over a hillside of stone steps with Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre across the valley. We bird the gardens and forest edge here on the evening of arrival and again on the way down.

    30 birds possible

    Birds: Spotted Forktail, Little Forktail, Brown Dipper, Plumbeous Water Redstart, White-capped Redstart, Blue Whistling-Thrush, Grey Wagtail, Crested Kingfisher, Mountain Bulbul, Great Barbet, Scarlet Minivet, Himalayan Bluetail, White-throated Fantail, Wallcreeper, Blue-fronted Redstart, Himalayan Bulbul, Oriental White-eye, Grey-headed Canary-Flycatcher, Rufous Sibia, Streaked Laughingthrush, Spotted Nutcracker, Hodgson's Redstart, Himalayan Griffon, Lammergeier, Eurasian Kestrel, Himalayan Monal, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Golden Eagle, Common Rosefinch

    Mammals: Himalayan Langur, Himalayan Tahr

    Plants: Tree Rhododendron

    Butterflies: Indian Tortoiseshell

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)

  6. Day 6Dovan

    Day 6→

    2,600 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. The trail drops to a river then climbs to Sinuwa, the doorway to the Modi Khola gorge. Birds come easily here at mid-morning as the clouds lift from the forest.

    2. Stay: teahouse. Simple teahouse in the bamboo forest of the Modi Khola gorge

      A damp, green gorge where bamboo walls the trail and the river is always roaring. Pheasants and partridges call at dawn and Himalayan Langur crash through the trees.

    20 birds possible

    Birds: Great Parrotbill, Rufous-gorgeted Flycatcher, Whiskered Yuhina, Stripe-throated Yuhina, Golden-breasted Fulvetta, White-browed Fulvetta, Grey-crested Tit, Rufous-bellied Woodpecker, Slaty-blue Flycatcher, Hill Partridge, Rufous-vented Yuhina, Chestnut-crowned Warbler, Spotted Laughingthrush, Black-faced Warbler, Himalayan Monal, Plumbeous Water Redstart, Satyr Tragopan, Koklass Pheasant, Himalayan Bluetail, Golden Bush-Robin

    Mammals: Himalayan Langur, Yellow-throated Marten, Himalayan Black Bear

    Seen from here: Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)

  7. Day 7Deurali

    Day 7→

    3,230 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. Stay: teahouse. Last teahouse before the high Sanctuary, below the Hinku cave and a huge cliff

      Via the Himalaya teahouse (2,920 m), we reach Deurali at the tree line. Here Himalayan Monals and Blood Pheasants share the slope with Himalayan Tahr on the cliffs, and the forest gives way to rhododendron scrub and rock.

    15 birds possible

    Birds: Rufous-breasted Accentor, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Rufous-vented Tit, White-browed Fulvetta, Himalayan Monal, Blood Pheasant, Alpine Accentor, Plain Mountain Finch, Yellow-billed Chough, Lammergeier, Himalayan Griffon, Dark-breasted Rosefinch, Spotted Nutcracker, Golden Eagle, Snow Pigeon

    Mammals: Himalayan Tahr, Blue Sheep

    Plants: Bell-flowered Rhododendron, Lepidote Rhododendron

    Seen from here: Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)

  8. Day 8Annapurna Base Camp

    Day 8→

    4,130 m · 1 night(s) · lodge

    1. We enter the Sanctuary through a gap between Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre. Snow Pigeons wheel along the cliffs and Blue Sheep graze the grass, an hour or two of easy walking before the final steps to Annapurna Base Camp.

    2. Stay: lodge. Basic lodge at the base camp; cold nights and warm dal bhat

      The end of the climb: a vast bowl of ice with Annapurna I, Annapurna South, Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre all around. We watch sunset and then dawn here, with Snow Pigeons, Lammergeier and Alpine Accentors for company.

    12 birds possible

    Birds: ★ Snow Pigeon, ★ Alpine Accentor, Plain Mountain Finch, ★ Yellow-billed Chough, Red-billed Chough, ★ Lammergeier, Himalayan Griffon, Himalayan Snowcock, Golden Eagle, Water Pipit, White-winged Redstart, Grandala

    Mammals: ★ Large-eared Pika, ★ Blue Sheep, Himalayan Tahr

    Plants: Drumstick Primula, Lepidote Rhododendron

    Butterflies: Indian Tortoiseshell

    Seen from here: Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m)

  9. Day 9Bamboo

    Day 9→

    2,335 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. Stay: teahouse. Teahouse in the bamboo forest; we passed through on day 6 and sleep here after the Sanctuary

      A long descent from the Sanctuary ends in the bamboo forest that gives the village its name. This is a rich evening stop for parrotbills, fulvettas, flycatchers and warblers, and a chance to compare notes on the day.

    17 birds possible

    Birds: Rufous-vented Yuhina, Whiskered Yuhina, Chestnut-crowned Warbler, Black-faced Warbler, Slaty-blue Flycatcher, ★ Great Parrotbill, ★ Golden-breasted Fulvetta, Nepal Fulvetta, ★ White-browed Fulvetta, Rufous-gorgeted Flycatcher, Spotted Laughingthrush, Plumbeous Water Redstart, Satyr Tragopan, Koklass Pheasant, Gold-naped Finch, ★ Himalayan Bluetail, ★ Golden Bush-Robin

    Mammals: Himalayan Langur, Yellow-throated Marten

    Seen from here: Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Annapurna South (7,219 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)

  10. Day 10Jhinu Danda

    Day 10→

    1,780 m · 1 night(s) · teahouse

    1. Stay: teahouse. Teahouse above the hot springs; a soak in the springs is the reward at the end of the day

      We drop back into warm, leafy forest and walk down to the natural hot springs on the Modi Khola. The river bank here is lovely for forktails, redstarts and the occasional Crested Kingfisher.

    15 birds possible

    Birds: Plumbeous Water Redstart, Grey Wagtail, Himalayan Bulbul, Great Barbet, Black Bulbul, Spotted Forktail, Little Forktail, Brown Dipper, Blue Whistling-Thrush, White-capped Redstart, Ultramarine Flycatcher, Verditer Flycatcher, Crested Kingfisher, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Hodgson's Redstart

    Mammals: Himalayan Langur, Yellow-throated Marten

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)

  11. Day 11Ghandruk

    Day 11→

    1,940 m · 1 night(s) · homestay

    1. A Gurung village on the hillside above the Modi Khola and a long lunch stop. The gardens and field edges are good for sunbirds, bulbuls and flycatchers.

    2. Stay: homestay. Gurung homestay or small lodge in the stone village, with mountain views

      A final night in one of Nepal's loveliest Gurung villages, with slate roofs and stone lanes. The oak forest on the edge of Ghandruk is great for a last morning flock, with Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre in the background.

    26 birds possible

    Birds: Himalayan Bulbul, Red-vented Bulbul, Oriental White-eye, Black-throated Sunbird, Great Barbet, Verditer Flycatcher, Scarlet Minivet, Long-tailed Minivet, Rufous Sibia, Spotted Dove, Rose-ringed Parakeet, Slaty-headed Parakeet, Red-billed Blue Magpie, Oriental Honey-buzzard, Streaked Laughingthrush, Whiskered Yuhina, Black-throated Tit, Eurasian Jay, Blue-winged Minla, Grey-hooded Warbler, Oriental Magpie-Robin, Spotted Nutcracker, Chestnut-crowned Laughingthrush, Yellow-billed Blue Magpie, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Himalayan Bluetail

    Mammals: Himalayan Langur

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)

  12. Day 12Nayapul

    Day 12→

    1,070 m

    1. The last real birding stop before the road, as the trail comes down to the valley floor and the river forest. Wagtails, kingfishers and flycatchers keep the walking leisurely.

    2. The road end where we began. A cup of tea, a last look at the river, and a few last bird ticks before the transfer back to Pokhara.

    14 birds possible

    Birds: Grey Wagtail, White Wagtail, Red-vented Bulbul, White-throated Kingfisher, Plumbeous Water Redstart, Brown Dipper, Verditer Flycatcher, Black Bulbul, Great Barbet, Crested Kingfisher, Common Kingfisher, Spotted Forktail, Black Kite, Oriental Magpie-Robin

    Butterflies: Common Mormon

    Seen from here: Annapurna South (7,219 m), Hiunchuli (6,441 m), Machhapuchhre (6,993 m), Annapurna I (8,091 m)
