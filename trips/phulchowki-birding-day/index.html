Kathmandu Birding Day: Phulchowki & Godawari

Kathmandu's best birding mountain in a single day: forested slopes at dawn, Godawari garden at midday.

1 day, up to 2,782 m, and 81 birds recorded on this route.

Best months: February to April and October to November.

Start the journeyDay by day, on the map

1 / 5

One full day on Phulchowki, the forested mountain on the rim of the Kathmandu Valley, and in the Godawari gardens below it. From oak and rhododendron forest up to the summit at 2,782 m: laughingthrushes, sunbirds, forktails at the temple stream and Nepal's only endemic bird, the Spiny Babbler. Easy walking, and ideal before or after a trek.

With guests

  • March 2026, Phulchowki Mai TempleLunch with a forktail

    with Placeholder Guest E and partner, United States

    We stopped at the Mai temple for lunch, unwrapped our sandwiches on the stone steps and, as always, I told my guests to keep their eyes on the stream. Within five minutes a Spotted Forktail was bobbing along the wet rocks a few metres from our feet, its long black-and-white tail swinging.

    A Rufous-bellied Niltava flicked in above us, and a White-capped Redstart appeared on a boulder in mid-stream. Nobody finished their sandwich for a good while. That is a perfect Phulchowki lunch.

    Seen that day: Spotted Forktail, Little Forktail, White-capped Redstart, Rufous-bellied Niltava, Large Niltava

  • November 2025, Phulchowki SummitA clear day in November

    with Placeholder Guest F and friends, Spain

    November is when the Kathmandu air is at its clearest, and the Langtang range stood out against the sky at the summit as if it were painted. A Himalayan Bluetail, blue-backed and orange-flanked, was working the undergrowth by the path, and my guests had their cameras trained on it from the moment we arrived.

    While we looked at the mountains, two Himalayan Griffons rose on a thermal right past the summit. One came so close we could hear the air in its feathers. I told them: remember this, this is Kathmandu's own mountain, and it is only an hour from the city.

    Seen that day: Himalayan Bluetail, Himalayan Griffon, Spotted Nutcracker, Black Eagle

  • Trip report, March 2026Forktails, sunbirds and a picnic by the stream

A day on this trip

I pick you up in Kathmandu around half past five, and we bird from dawn up through the forest to the summit, have a picnic lunch by the temple stream and spend the afternoon at Godawari. Two or three hours of easy walking in all, and you're back in the city by five.

The day, stop by stop

  1. Lower Phulchowki Forest1,750 m · Spiny Babbler, Himalayan Cutia

    1. We depart Kathmandu before sunrise (around 5:30–6:00 AM) to reach the birding sites while activity is highest, with a packed breakfast and hot tea or coffee provided en route. Then we drive up the forested slopes of Phulchowki, stopping at productive spots along the road. Look for laughingthrushes, minivets, yuhinas, niltavas and, with luck, the elusive Spiny Babbler, Nepal's only endemic bird.

    Birds 27: Rufous Sibia, Whiskered Yuhina, Blue-winged Minla, White-throated Laughingthrush, Great Barbet, Black Bulbul, Chestnut-crowned Laughingthrush, Striated Laughingthrush, Rufous-chinned Laughingthrush, Rufous-winged Fulvetta, Nepal Fulvetta, Yellow-billed Blue Magpie, Green-tailed Sunbird, Black-throated Sunbird, Mountain Bulbul, Greater Yellownape, Large Niltava, Slaty-blue Flycatcher, Long-tailed Minivet, Himalayan Cutia, Mrs. Gould's Sunbird, Golden-throated Barbet, Speckled Wood-Pigeon, Ashy Wood-Pigeon, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Grey-winged Blackbird, Spiny Babbler

    Mammals 1: Assamese Macaque

    Butterflies 1: Northern Jungle Queen

    Plants 1: Tree Rhododendron

  2. Upper Oak–Rhododendron Forest2,500 m · Spotted Laughingthrush, Great Parrotbill

    1. Higher up the road, the forest turns cool and tall, with rhododendrons, orchids on the oak limbs and the chance of a Spotted Laughingthrush or Great Parrotbill in the bamboo. Flowers draw sunbirds in the spring.

    Birds 20: Rufous-vented Tit, Grey-crested Tit, Spot-winged Tit, Stripe-throated Yuhina, Bar-tailed Treecreeper, Spotted Laughingthrush, Hoary-throated Barwing, Chestnut-tailed Minla, Rufous-gorgeted Flycatcher, Yellow-browed Tit, Spotted Nutcracker, Fire-tailed Sunbird, Mrs. Gould's Sunbird, Scarlet Minivet, Great Parrotbill, Koklass Pheasant, Himalayan Cutia, Golden-breasted Fulvetta, Blue-fronted Redstart, Collared Grosbeak

    Mammals 2: Barking Deer, Yellow-throated Marten

    Plants 3: Tree Rhododendron, Bearded Rhododendron, Necklace Orchid

  3. Phulchowki Summit2,782 m · Black Eagle, Himalayan Bluetail

    1. By mid-morning we reach the summit at 2,782 m, with a wide view across the Kathmandu Valley and, on a clear day, the white wall of the Langtang and Jugal Himal. Raptors start to circle in the thermals as the day warms.

    Birds 10: ★ Spotted Nutcracker (seen with guests on this trip), White-throated Laughingthrush, ★ Himalayan Bluetail (seen with guests on this trip), ★ Black Eagle (seen with guests on this trip), ★ Himalayan Griffon (seen with guests on this trip), Egyptian Vulture, Mountain Hawk-Eagle, Rufous-vented Tit, Blue-fronted Redstart, Oriental Honey-buzzard

    Plants 1: Bearded Rhododendron

  4. Phulchowki Mai Temple2,100 m · Little Forktail, Red-headed Trogon

    1. On the way down we stop for a packed lunch at the little temple, where a rushing stream cuts through the forest. Forktails and redstarts bob on the wet rocks, while niltavas and thrushes feed in the shady clearing.

    Birds 20: ★ Spotted Forktail (seen with guests on this trip), ★ White-capped Redstart (seen with guests on this trip), Plumbeous Water Redstart, ★ Rufous-bellied Niltava (seen with guests on this trip), Yellow-bellied Fantail, Grey-hooded Warbler, ★ Little Forktail (seen with guests on this trip), ★ Large Niltava (seen with guests on this trip), Chestnut-bellied Rock-Thrush, Grey-winged Blackbird, Long-tailed Minivet, Whiskered Yuhina, Green-tailed Sunbird, Blue Whistling-Thrush, Collared Owlet, Red-headed Trogon, Himalayan Cutia, Rufous-gorgeted Flycatcher, Golden Bush-Robin, Himalayan Bluetail

    Mammals 1: Yellow-throated Marten

  5. National Botanical Garden, Godawari1,500 m · Himalayan Bulbul, Red-vented Bulbul

    1. After lunch we continue to Godawari Botanical Garden, where mixed forest and open lawns attract flycatchers, barbets, woodpeckers and sunbirds. We wrap up the checklist over tea before dropping you back at your hotel by early evening (around 5:00–6:00 PM).

    Birds 28: Himalayan Bulbul, Black Bulbul, Red-vented Bulbul, Great Barbet, Oriental White-eye, Black-throated Tit, Grey-hooded Warbler, Green-backed Tit, Red-billed Blue Magpie, Scarlet Minivet, Verditer Flycatcher, Rufous-bellied Niltava, Little Pied Flycatcher, Blue Whistling-Thrush, Streaked Laughingthrush, Eurasian Jay, Oriental Magpie-Robin, Fulvous-breasted Woodpecker, Grey-capped Pygmy Woodpecker, Blue-throated Barbet, Kalij Pheasant, Spotted Forktail, Grey-chinned Minivet, Common Kingfisher, Spiny Babbler, Ultramarine Flycatcher, Himalayan Bluetail, Hodgson's Redstart

    Butterflies 2: Krishna Peacock, Common Mormon

    Plants 1: Himalayan Daphne
