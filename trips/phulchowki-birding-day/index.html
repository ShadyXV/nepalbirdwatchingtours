Phulchowki Birding Day Hike

Kathmandu's best birding mountain: one dawn-to-dusk day from valley garden to forested summit.

Phulchowki is the highest hill ringing the Kathmandu Valley and the best place to meet Himalayan forest birds in a single day. Krishna collects you at first light and we bird our way up from the Godavari botanical garden through oak, laurel and rhododendron forest to the summit at 2,782 m. On a good day you will see sunbirds, laurel-forest laughingthrushes, niltavas and forktails, and in spring the whole slope is bright with rhododendron. It is gentle on the legs and generous with birds.

Highlights

  • Dawn in the Godavari botanical garden, with flycatchers, minivets and Red-billed Blue Magpies
  • Laughingthrushes, minlas and Himalayan Cutia in the mossy middle forest
  • Streams with Spotted Forktail, Little Forktail and White-capped Redstart
  • Rhododendron arboreum in bloom from February to April
  • Summit views of the Langtang and Jugal Himal on clear days
  • A comfortable day: transport up the road, short flat walks, and plenty of time for photos

The way, day by day

  1. Day 1Phulchowki Summit

    Day 1→

    2,782 m

    1. Dawn start at the Godavari garden gate at about 06:00, when the paths are quiet and the first flocks are moving. An easy two hours of flat walking gives flycatchers, bulbuls, minivets and the day's first laughingthrushes.

    2. Mid-morning, we drive up to the first bends of the Phulchowki road and walk slowly through the mossy forest. This is the best stretch of the day for mixed flocks, with yuhinas, minlas, fulvettas and sibias often all in the same tree.

    3. We stop for a picnic lunch at the little temple around midday, where a rushing stream cuts through the forest. Forktails and redstarts bob on the wet rocks, while niltavas and thrushes feed in the shady clearing.

    4. Early afternoon in the cool upper forest, with tall rhododendrons, orchids on the oak limbs and the chance of a Spotted Laughingthrush or Great Parrotbill in the bamboo. Flowers draw sunbirds in the spring.

    5. We finish at the top by mid-afternoon, with a wide view across the Kathmandu Valley and, on a clear day, the white wall of the Langtang and Jugal Himal. Raptors circle in the thermals before we drift back down, birding the forest on the way and reaching Kathmandu by early evening.
